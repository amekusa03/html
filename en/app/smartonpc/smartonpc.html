Integrate Generic ESP32-C3 into your Ubuntu PC to meet smart home standardsMatterThis is a project that corresponds to ``. Step 1 — Power control

function means
Power on Mechanical relay → Short press of power button (500ms)

Factory reset GPIO9 long press (3 seconds)
  • WoL (Wake on LAN) is not adopted — I tried to set up this PC, but it didn't work.
  • ESP32 - ○○ command → power off on PC — Not considered because it is no different from operating a PC in the first place
  • Power OFF: Short press the power button on the relay and activate Ubuntu's ACPI Settings (HandlePowerKey=poweroff) initiates a graceful shutdown

Step 2 — Power status monitoring

function means
Power status detection GPIO input for power LED via PC817 (active LOW)
Deviation detection 2 seconds polling, confirmed 3 times in a row
Matter Attribute Sync When the deviation is confirmed attribute::update() Adjust the OnOff attribute to reality with
User notification Via Matter subscription (automatically received by Google Home, etc.)

fail-safe principle: Even if a deviation is detected, the system does not perform automatic ON/OFF operation. The actual situation is reflected in the Matter attribute and left to the user.

Hardware configuration

Main parts

  • Microcomputer: Generic ESP32-C3
  • host PC: Ubuntu
  • power control: Short circuit PWR SW pin of motherboard with single channel relay module (ASIN: B0G4C9GMVH)
  • Power status detection: Read power LED via PC817 optocoupler
  • controller: Google Nest Hub / Android smartphone
  • Power supply: Constant power supply from PC USB port

GPIO allocation

GPIO role circuit
GPIO 20 OUTPUT: PWR SW control Via mechanical relay
GPIO 4 INPUT: PWR LED detection Via PC817 photocoupler (active LOW)
GPIO 9 INPUT: Factory reset Internal pull-up, LOW=press

circuit diagram

PWR SW (GPIO 20 → mechanical relay → motherboard)

GPIO 20 → Relay control input Relay contact → Motherboard PWR SW header short circuit

PWR LED (Motherboard → PC817 → GPIO 4)

PWR_LED+ → 470Ω → PC817 Anode(pin1) PC817 Cathode(pin2) → PWR_LED- (GND) 3.3V → 10kΩ → PC817 Collector(pin4) → GPIO 4 PC817 Emitter(pin3) → GND PC ON: LED lit → PC817 continuity → GPIO = LOW PC OFF: LED off → PC817 shut off → GPIO = HIGH (pullup)

The polarity is pc_control.c of PWR_SW_ACTIVE_HIGH Can be switched using source code.

design decisions

BLE disable/WiFi only commissioning

sdkconfig.defaults BLE is completely disabled (CONFIG_BT_ENABLED=n, CONFIG_ENABLE_CHIPOBLE=n）。

reason: store_wifi_credentials() Because we hardcode the WiFi credentials in , the device attempts to connect to WiFi as soon as it boots. If CHIPoBLE (Matter's BLE advertisement) operates in this state, WiFi connection may be inhibited due to BLE/WiFi radio conflict. In designs that pre-embed WiFi credentials, disabling BLE and commissioning via IP network (kOnNetwork) are unified.

Fail-safe principle (operation when deviation is detected)

The premise of the Matter specification is that ``the actual situation can be determined definitively.'' Automatic ON/OFF in the event of deviation creates a risk of malfunction.

Safe direction = no operation. The decision is left to the user.

Behavior when the discrepancy is confirmed (no discrepancy after 3 consecutive polls):

  1. attribute::update() Sync the OnOff attribute to reality with
  2. Notify controllers (Google Home, etc.) via Matter subscription
  3. This system does not operate the power supply even with the information obtained.

Known issues/issues

mDNS conflict (resolved)

In the old implementation (Ping-based aliveness monitoring), ESP-IDF mdns_init() and Matter internal mDNS were conflicting on port 5353. Currently, mDNS is not used as it has been replaced with power LED polling using GPIO. The problem has been resolved.

Freeze automatic recovery (not implemented)

Currently, we have not detected cases where the power LED remains on and the PC does not respond (freezes). Manual action required. Users need to understand the system and take appropriate actions.

Restrictions

  • This project assumes a typical home router environment at home.
  • In networks that are strictly managed by authentication servers or networks that do not have sufficient security management, Your use of Matter may be restricted.
