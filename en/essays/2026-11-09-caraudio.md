# After Failing to Create a Car Time Alignment App: Rebuilding AndroidSoundTune

2026-11-09

## Car Audio Tuning

I just can't seem to get the sound of my car audio system right.
As I've mentioned in other articles, I've been making gradual improvements, but there are limits to adjusting the system based solely on my ears and intuition.

So, I decided to revisit the Android app (`AndroidSoundTune`) I had created earlier.
To be precise, since I had tried to incorporate features like time alignment measurement last time and ended up turning it into a black box—rendering it completely unusable—I’m rebuilding it from scratch.

This time, rather than attempting to measure distances that were beyond our capabilities, we focused on implementing two features designed to provide a visual understanding of the “characteristics” of the speakers and the in-car environment.

## Feature 1: Visualization of Frequency Response Using a Logarithmic Sweep

This feature plays a sweep tone, captures the sound with your smartphone's microphone, and displays the results as a graph.
By playing the sweep tone through your home audio system or car stereo and taking measurements, you can "visualize" the frequency response for each environment.

In addition, we’ve made it possible to overlay and compare multiple graphs. This allows you to see at a glance which frequency bands in your car’s audio are weaker or stronger compared to your home audio system.

## Function 2: Reverberation (After-sound) Measurement

Another feature measures the "reverberation" of sound.
It outputs a 500-millisecond sine wave, picks it up with a microphone, and measures how long it takes for the sound to fade away, thereby assessing the reverberation (residual sound) inside the vehicle.

The goal is to identify the frequency bands where standing waves or unwanted reflections are occurring and use that information as a guide for equalizer adjustments and sound deadening.
