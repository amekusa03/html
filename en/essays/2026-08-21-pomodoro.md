# Pomodoro Technique: Tailor your concentration and breaks to your own specifications

2026-08-21

I was looking into ways to deal with stress and how I use my time and came across something called the Pomodoro Technique.
I won't go into details, but the gist is a simple time management technique of 25 minutes of work followed by a 5-minute break.

I tried looking for an app that would work reasonably well in the Ubuntu environment, but I couldn't find anything that suited me. ``Then let's make it,'' I started writing with a lighthearted feeling.
However, once I started making it, I had to worry about practical interruptions and operational considerations, such as ``Wouldn't it be a nuisance if a notification dialog suddenly appeared while I was concentrating on typing?'' and ``What would I do if it coincided with my scheduled break at work?'' It turned out to be more in-depth than I expected.

In the end, I decided to create a Pomodoro timer for Linux called "FocusCycle" using Qt5 (C++), incorporating my own interpretation and commitment.

## my own interpretation

Rather than simply measuring 25 minutes mechanically, we have incorporated the following interpretations and specifications so that it can be used comfortably at actual work sites.

- **Wait for task completion while operating (intensive protection)**: To avoid distracting your concentration while operating the keyboard or mouse, wait a few seconds until there is no operation (idle state) before displaying the dialog.
- **Flexible change of time**: Instead of a fixed 25 minutes, you can change the time in settings to suit your work style.
- **Prevent automatic progress after a break**: Requires explicit user interaction from the end of a break to the start of the next task. Prevent the count from advancing automatically when you are absent.
- **Unobtrusive UI that prioritizes work**: Compact UI with circular progression ring, placed so as not to get in the way of work. You can now check the status and elapsed time details by right-clicking.

## overview

**FocusCycle** is a modern and lightweight Pomodoro Technique practice desktop application developed in **Qt5 (C++)** for Ubuntu / Linux environments. It supports Wayland / X11 compatible inactivity time detection function, company break time schedule function, automatic recording of work history, resident system tray, and display switching between English and Japanese.

## Main features

- **Pomodoro Timer Engine**: Flexibly customize work time (1 minute to 55 minutes) and break time (1 minute to 15 minutes).
- **Intensive protection (operation detection function)**:
  - Threshold detection of inactivity time (0 seconds to 30 seconds).
  - Prefer Wayland environment (GNOME Mutter D-Bus API: `org.gnome.Mutter.IdleMonitor`) and use XScreenSaver as fallback for X11.
  - The display of dialogs is automatically suppressed while the user is concentrating on operating the PC, and a break dialog is displayed when the amount of time the keyboard and mouse have not been used reaches the set value.
- **Company break time schedule**:
  - You can register up to 10 regular break times (example: 12:00, 15:00).
  - When the set time is reached, a special dialog will be displayed, allowing you to choose whether to take a break immediately or continue working.
- **Compact & draggable UI**:
  - Chic dark theme with circular countdown prog wrestling.
  - Compact design allows you to move freely on your desktop by dragging anywhere on the card.
- **System tray resident function**: Work completion/break completion operations, setting dialog launch, log confirmation, real-time display in tooltips.
- **Automatic recording of work log**: Automatically save Pomodoro execution history in CSV format (`~/.local/share/FocusCycle/history.csv`).
- **Multilingual UI support**: You can switch between English (default) and Japanese at any time from the settings dialog.
