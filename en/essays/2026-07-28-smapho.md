# Smartphones you can buy for 30,000 yen, things you can't underestimate

Be honest. Smartphones these days are too expensive. Under such circumstances, I thought that smartphones under 30,000 yen were ``cheap or bad''. My understanding was that it was either a student's first phone or a post-graduation model for kids' phones.

But when I looked into it, the situation was quite different. This price range is called the ``entry class,'' and it seems to be the volume zone that attracts the most realistic customers who don't want to play games or take professional-grade photos, and just want LINE, the Web, SNS, and videos to work without any problems. This may be an interesting area where the true feelings of people who don't put on pretense are reflected.

Each one uses a different philosophy, so the character of the person making the choice will be tested.

## New or used former champion?

There is an interesting turning point here. If you're aiming for this price range, a **used former high-end machine** is also a common option.

People who choose a new entry-level machine are probably the type who value "safety, security, and longevity." The battery does not deteriorate, the support for the latest OS is guaranteed for several years, and the manufacturer's warranty covers initial defects. It's a solid choice.

On the other hand, people who reach for used former high-end devices are the type who prioritize "performance, camera, and comfort." It's not uncommon for flagships from a few years ago to have more powerful processing power than today's cheap new cameras, and the underlying capabilities of the cameras, such as night view mode and image stabilization, are also different. The good color reproduction of the organic EL display is also something you can't enjoy on entry-level models.

In other words, this is the same problem as the eternal question of whether it's a new light car or an outdated luxury car. Even in the low price, there is a philosophical conflict.

## The minimum level to avoid failure

However, no matter which option you choose, there are some things you should not miss.

- **Memory (RAM) is 6GB or more**. With 4GB, apps as of 2026 may feel sluggish.
- **Storage is over 128GB**. It's best to avoid 64GB, as it will quickly fill up with photos and apps.
- **Presence or absence of Osaifu-Keitai (FeliCa)**. Some ultra-cheap models from overseas manufacturers are not equipped with it.
- **Waterproof performance**. If you plan on using it on rainy days or in the kitchen, a rating of IPX5/8 or higher is recommended.

Even if it's less than 30,000 yen, if you compromise on this, you'll end up wasting your money by buying something cheap. On the other hand, if you stick to this line, you can live more comfortably in this price range than you think.

The wisest decisions may be hidden in the choices that are not pretentious.
