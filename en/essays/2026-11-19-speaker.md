# The Car Audio Rabbit Hole, Part 3: DIY Starts with Replacing Speakers

## Why We Recommend These Speakers

Since my last post ended up being a bit of a lecture, I’d like to talk about some concrete practical tips this time.

When it comes to DIY car audio projects, the first step I’d most highly recommend is “replacing the speakers.”
There’s plenty of information online about disassembly procedures and installation for each car model, and above all, you can clearly hear the difference in sound quality before and after the replacement. It’s the project where you’ll most easily see the value for your money and feel a real sense of accomplishment.

Of course, having a car shop handle it is a sure bet, but it can also be fun to take the plunge and try doing it yourself while following online guides and videos.
If you get stuck or feel discouraged along the way, you can always turn to a professional shop or dealer at that point. As I mentioned in the title of my previous post, if you have “courage and insurance,” things tend to work out surprisingly well.

## Coaxial or Separate?

When you start researching speakers to choose one, the first two types you’ll come across are “coaxial” and “separate.”

A coaxial speaker is a type in which a tweeter for high frequencies is integrated into the center of a woofer that produces low frequencies. All you have to do is remove the door panel and swap it out for the stock speaker—it’s so easy that the installation is practically a “plug-and-play” process.

Separate speakers, on the other hand, are a type in which the woofer and tweeter are separate units. Although it takes some effort to install the tweeter on the dashboard or near the pillars and run the wiring there, it allows you to hear vocals and instruments coming from right in front of you. Coaxial speakers are definitely the easier choice, but if you’re looking for sound localization and a sense of spaciousness, the separate-component system is the clear winner.

……That said, there’s also an unconventional path into this hobby—like mine—where you “start by installing a simple coaxial speaker, then get greedy and buy a separate tweeter to add later.”

## The "Baffle" and "Connector": Unassuming but Essential Components

In addition to the speaker itself, you’ll want to keep in mind the inner baffle (mounting base) and the adapter connector.

Since the shapes of screw holes in door panels and the specifications of wiring connectors vary by automaker, these adapters play a crucial role in making off-the-shelf speakers compatible with your vehicle. While new speakers often come with universal mounting brackets included, caution is advised when purchasing used speakers individually on platforms like Yahoo! Auctions or flea market apps, as these brackets are frequently missing.

There are also many high-end baffles available for separate purchase that prioritize rigidity and sound quality (such as those made of high-density MDF or metal). I once tried replacing mine with a “slightly better baffle,” but… unfortunately, my ears couldn’t detect any dramatic improvement. Experiencing this harsh reality is part of the thrill of DIY.

## Budget and Options: From 10,000 yen to the Ultra-High-End

As for the budget—which is a common concern—it really varies widely.
That said, even with an entry-level model that retails for around 10,000 yen, the sound will be noticeably more defined compared to the flimsy, stock paper cone speakers, and you should be able to experience more than enough of an improvement.

If you're looking at domestic manufacturers, you can't go wrong with Pioneer (Carrozzeria), Kenwood, Clarion, or Alpine. (Units from overseas manufacturers favored by audiophiles are also appealing, but since delving into them gets a bit too niche, I'll skip them here.)

By the way, if you’re the kind of person who “spares no expense,” you certainly won’t regret choosing Carrozzeria’s flagship model, [TS-Z1000RS](https://jpn.pioneer/ja/carrozzeria/speaker/highend_sp/ts-rs1000/?ref=header).
…That said, with a list price of roughly 490,000 yen (tax included), we’re talking about a price range where you could practically buy a used car just with the speakers alone.

## Precisely because it's the last exit

In an audio system, the speaker is the “final output” that converts electrical signals into air vibrations, and above all, it is the component located closest to the human ear. For that very reason, it offers exceptional value in terms of careful selection and the joy of customizing it.

The moment you choose the speakers you like, peel off the interior trim, and replace them, the door to the true “car audio rabbit hole” quietly swings open.
