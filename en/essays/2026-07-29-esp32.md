# This is next! Standard for electronic work “ESP32”

## 　

![alt text](/en/essays/2026-07-29-esp32.png)

If you've ever tried "L Chika" with Arduino Uno, connected a sensor, and grinned to yourself, "Wow, it worked," you're probably thinking this by now.

"I want to do something more complicated" "I want to do something wireless"

What stands in the way of that desire is Arduino's ``lack of specs.'' I myself am one of the people who hit this wall.

In the past, Arduino was the gateway to electronic work, but now in 2026, that position has shifted to ESP32. Wireless modules that once cost several thousand yen can now be packaged entirely on a chip that costs a few hundred yen, and their processing power is on an order of magnitude. This single piece solves the problems of ``lack of memory'' and ``spaghetti wiring'' that plague beginners.

---

## 1. The difference in specifications is too “another dimension”

If you want to talk about ESP32, I want you to look at these numbers first.

| Item | Arduino Uno | ESP32-WROOM |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CPU (Number of Cores)** | 16MHz (Single) | Maximum 240MHz (Dual) |
| **SRAM (memory)** | 2KB | Approx. 520KB |
| **Flash memory** | 32KB | 4MB or more |
| **Wireless function** | None | Wi-Fi / Bluetooth included |

The clock is 15 times faster, and the memory is more than 100 times faster. This is no longer "evolution" but "another dimension."

Moreover, what personally impresses me is that ESP32-WROOM is a dual core (Xtensa LX6). One core handles Wi-Fi communication, while the other handles the main processing. Because of this extra margin, Arduino is able to achieve high-definition LCD displays and real-time control that handles multiple processes at the same time, with a chip costing just a few hundred yen. It's a bit of an oddity, being pocket-friendly yet luxurious.

---

## 2. I am grateful that Wi-Fi and Bluetooth are included from the beginning.

The ESP32 is not just a microcontroller, but a ``SoC (System on Chip)'' that also includes communication functions. The fact that there is no need to purchase a separate Wi-Fi shield is quite effective.

One trend to keep in mind in 2026 is compliance with the next-generation smart home standard ``Matter.'' In particular, the ESP32-C6 is also compatible with Wi-Fi 6 and Zigbee/Thread, making it the core of IoT development that seamlessly connects devices from different manufacturers.

Actually, I also have the experience of trying to make an OBD-II data logger using this ESP32-C6. This is a self-made device that collects vehicle information when combined with ELM327. In the end, I settled on an ESP32 development board made by Ideaspark, but the ease of having wireless and display on one board was unimaginable in the Arduino era. My first-hand experience has shown me that it is no exaggeration to say that it is possible to take a prototype to mass production level. The board has actually been commercialized.

## 3. Rules unique to Japan that are illegal if you don't know them

I've talked about the appeal so far, but as long as it's used in Japan, there are rules that can't be ignored. This is the "Technical Standards Conformity Certification (Technical Compliance)".

Devices that emit radio waves are regulated by law, and if you use a device without a technical compliance mark in Japan without permission, you may be subject to heavy penalties such as up to one year in prison or a fine of up to 1 million yen. You can't just say "I didn't know", so please be sure to keep the following points in mind when purchasing.

- **Choose a certified module such as "ESP-WROOM-32"**: Designed and certified for legal use in Japan.
- **Check points when purchasing**:
  - Is there a mention of "Technical Competency Qualified" in the product image or description on Amazon, etc.?
  - Use reliable, long-established domestic retailers such as Akizuki Electronics Trading and Switch Science.
  - Check with the actual chip to see if there is a logo that looks like a postal mark and a certification number such as "R211-XXXXXX" engraved on the metal shield on the surface of the chip.

## 4. The development environment has become surprisingly friendly.

Honestly, I understand the concern that ``the more sophisticated the microcontroller, the more difficult it seems to configure.'' However, Arduino IDE 2.x in 2026 has evolved dramatically, and now auto-completion and fast compilation have become commonplace, creating an environment where beginners can get started without stress.

The first stumbling block for beginners is usually ``writing errors''. Just hold on here and you'll be fine.

1. **Add board manager URL**: Enter the latest URL recommended by Espressif in the basic settings and install the package.
2. **Golden Steps for Writing**: When "Connecting..." appears on the console at the bottom of the screen, don't panic and go to the next step.

### What to do when "Connecting..." is displayed

- Press and hold the **BOOT button** on the board.
- As soon as writing (percentage display) begins, release your finger.

Even if the board has an auto-reset circuit, this "manual BOOT" is the best insurance. I was also saved by this once.

## 5. Choose a “partner” according to what you want to make

As of 2026, there are various derivative models of ESP32. It is best to choose one according to your purpose.

- **For serious research/edge AI**: ESP32-S3/WROOM
  - Take full advantage of the power of dual cores. Built-in AI accelerator also supports image recognition.
- **For small IoT/battery powered**: XIAO ESP32C3
  - Ultra-compact, low power consumption model that uses RISC-V architecture. Directly connected to USB-C for easy writing.
- **For easy prototyping and learning**: M5StickC Plus
  - The liquid crystal, battery, and sensor are packaged from the beginning, making it suitable for when you want to quickly create something.

### When building a robot car, the power supply design will determine the outcome.

If you plan to run a motor with ESP32, this is really important.

- **Power supply separation**: For the motor, prepare a high-output battery such as NCR18650B (2S/8.4V), and for the microcontroller, prepare 5V through a step-down converter such as LM2596.
- **Strict Voltage Control**: Always adjust the output of the LM2596 to 5.0V with a tester before connecting it to the ESP32. The moment 6V or more flows, the chip says goodbye.
- **Gold rule for common ground**: Even if the power supply system is divided, only the GND (negative side) must be connected to one wire. If you forget this, the signal will not pass and you will lose control. I once suffered from an unexplained freeze for half a day.
