# The Car Audio Rabbit Hole, Part 9: The Sacred Realm Known as Speaker Cables

2026-11-24

## I'll come clean right off the bat

After power cables, the next topic is, of course, “speaker cables.”
…But before I get to the main point, there’s something I need to admit honestly.

I'm sorry, but when it comes to this field, I don't have enough in-depth knowledge to go around spouting off like some know-it-all.

In the world of pure audio, speaker cables are a “sacred realm (or a realm of chaos)” that can sometimes even spark religious-like debates. They range from cables costing a few hundred yen per meter to ultra-high-end, super-expensive cables that could buy you a used compact car, and heated debates rage day and night over whether they’re made of “single-crystal copper” “silver-plated,” “no, pure silver wire”—heated debates rage day and night.
For a humble DIY enthusiast like me to casually set foot in such a world would be nothing short of reckless.

## Results from Testing the Popular "Belden 8460"

That said, I, too, am someone who has dipped a toe into the car audio rabbit hole.
Unable to resist the curiosity of “Does the cable really make a difference in sound?”, I purchased the “Belden 8460”—a cable renowned as a staple in studios and live music venues—and actually rewired my system with it. It’s a twisted-pair cable with white and black wires twisted into a spiral—it looks like the kind of professional-grade equipment you’d find in a studio.

When I compared it to a very ordinary OFC (oxygen-free copper) cable sold at an auto parts store… as frustrating as it is to admit, there really is a difference.

I wouldn’t go so far as to say the sound changed dramatically. However, I could definitely sense a change in the sound’s character—the vocals had gained a richer, more lustrous quality, and the high-pitched cymbals cut through with greater clarity.
It was a moment when I truly realized that when it comes to cables, it’s not so much a matter of “the more expensive, the better,” but rather a world shaped by the unique characteristics of your speakers and amplifier—and, above all, whether they suit your personal taste.

## The Harsh Reality Unique to Cars

…Having said that, you might be thinking, “Well, then, I’ll go buy a bunch of different cables and compare how they sound!”
But hold on a second. With home audio systems, you can easily try out all sorts of cables just by snapping them into the terminals on the back of your amp.

However, this is “inside a car.”
If you try to replace the speaker cables inside the car, you have to rip off the interior panels, peel back the floor carpet, and—the biggest challenge of all—use a thin piece of wire as a guide to thread the cable through the **“accordion-style rubber hose (boot)” connecting the door to the body”**, all while working up a sweat.

This task is truly hellishly grueling.
Just shoving my hand into that narrow gap, scraping my fingers raw, and getting covered in grease just to thread the two front wires through is enough to make me want to give up. If just the front doors are this much of an ordeal, the mere thought of having to reroute the cables all the way to the rear speakers through every seat makes me feel like I’m going to pass out. There’s absolutely no way I could pull off a feat like “rerouting multiple cables and comparing the sound.”

## A Speculation Made Possible by Today's DSP Technology

If you ask me whether it’s really worth going to such great lengths just to be particular about cables, I’m personally a bit skeptical.
That’s because we now have a powerful tool at our disposal: “DSP.”

My honest guess is that if the subtle changes in sound—such as the clarity of the high frequencies or the richness of the midrange—that you achieve by going to the trouble of changing cables can be obtained by adjusting the DSP’s parametric equalizer by 0.5 dB or fine-tuning the crossover settings, wouldn’t that be a much safer and more reliable way to achieve the exact sound you’re aiming for?

## But the fact that I still can't help but get involved is what makes it such a trap.

That said, it’s also true that the mere fact that “that Belden cable—the one favored by pros—runs through the door of my beloved car” gives me a little boost of excitement while I’m driving.
Speaker cables, caught between the occult and science, and between self-satisfaction and manual labor.

If you have plenty of energy to spare on your day off and are prepared to endure some pain in your fingertips… it might be fun to give it a try at least once.
