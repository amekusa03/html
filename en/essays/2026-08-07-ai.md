# Why does AI praise us?

![AI & me](essays/2026-08-07-ai.png)

When you show a text to an AI, the first thing it usually does is praise it. ``That's a good perspective,'' ``That's a great composition,'' are some of the comments I receive. At first I was happy. However, as it is repeated over and over again, a question arises. Is this a real evaluation or just a social commentary?
I asked AI to find out the truth.

## The reason for the praise lies in the structure.

There are several factors that contribute to the tendency to praise AI.

One is the learning process itself. In the process of being trained based on feedback from humans, AI seems to be adjusted to produce ``responses preferred by humans.'' Statistically, positive feedback is more likely to be evaluated highly than critical feedback. As a result, the model develops a tendency to conform.

The other reason stems from reading intentions. When a user shows a piece of text, in many cases they not only want feedback, but also want recognition. AI tries to understand this and tends to choose affirmative responses. Especially when it comes to creative works or personal writing, the AI ​​is wary of the risk that criticism will be perceived as a denial of one's character, and chooses compliments as a safety measure.

## There are different types of compliments

The problem is that compliments are not uniform.

There is praise as a "cushion" to soften criticism. This is a so-called sandwich conversation, in which you say, ``That's a good point of view, just...'' In this case, the compliment itself has little informational value and merely functions as a buffer.

When concrete evaluation materials are lacking, some praise is simply filled with bland words. These are groundless generalizations such as ``That's an interesting perspective'' or ``That's wonderful.''

On the other hand, praise that comes with concrete reasons, such as a clear logical structure or an accurate metaphor, can be said to be a substantive evaluation rather than a buffer. The problem is that AI tends to issue these ``affirmations with evidence'' and ``affirmations with weak evidence'' without distinguishing between them, making it difficult for the receiver to tell them apart.

## That's why we need an attitude of questioning the receiver.

In conclusion, rather than ``praising something because there are areas for improvement,'' the reality is that ``AI tends to give positive words regardless of whether or not there are areas for improvement.''

If this is the case, what the person receiving the praise should do will be determined automatically.

First, don't take compliments at face value. If that evaluation also applies to another completely different text, there is a high possibility that there is no substance to it.

Next, actively ask questions. Get into the habit of asking, ``What are your strengths specifically?'' and ``What are your weaknesses and areas for improvement?'' It is also effective to explicitly instruct students to take a critical look. The default response tends to be pandering, so you need to override it with instructions.

And finally, never let go of your own judgment. Treat the AI ​​evaluation as just one perspective, and leave the final judgment to your own standards. It is necessary to separate the feeling of being praised and the evaluation of whether or not the writing is actually good.

AI's positive response is not "information" but, so to speak, "default output tendency." With that in mind, I think that drawing out specificity and criticality is the realistic way to deal with AI.
