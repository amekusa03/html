# Putting the MUJI, C3, C6, and S3 Through Their Paces: My “Personal Favorite ESP32”—What I Settled On After Trying All Sorts of Things

2026-11-23

## Let me get straight to the point: My top pick is the “ESP32-C3.”

There are plenty of detailed spec comparison charts and benchmark articles on the ESP32 series out there. If you're looking for the fine details on clock speeds, SRAM capacity, or supported wireless standards, you can find them all at a glance on those explanatory websites.

So, rather than getting into complicated numerical comparisons here, I’d like to talk about my “intuitive feel” based on my hands-on experience with various DIY projects.

Without beating around the bush, I’ll get straight to the point: after trying out various options, my “top pick”—the one I like best—is the **ESP32-C3**.

Why did I end up choosing the seemingly unassuming C3 instead of a high-spec, latest-generation model? I’d like to take a brief look back at my journey with the ESP32 that led me to this point.

## I chose the “C6 (with LCD)” because I wanted to feel tangible results.

Actually, the first thing I bought was an ESP32-C6 development board with a built-in color LCD (liquid crystal display).

Back then, I didn’t have a specific goal in mind—I just really wanted to build something. It all started out of sheer curiosity; I just wanted to try out the latest microcontroller.

However, it’s obvious that simply making an LED on a circuit board blink on and off (the so-called “L-chika”) without any specific purpose will quickly become boring and feel empty. After all, people naturally want graphical feedback that they can see right in front of them.

I had braced myself, thinking, “Controlling the LCD might be a bit of a challenge,” but when I actually tried it out, I found that modern development environments come with a truly comprehensive set of libraries. It wasn’t as if everything was easy by any means, but thanks to the wisdom of those who came before me, I didn’t hit any major roadblocks, and I felt genuinely excited when text and shapes suddenly appeared on the screen.

After I’d had my fill of playing around with the LCD and was satisfied with the results, I naturally felt motivated to “try building a simpler, more practical system.”

## The "Standard WROOM" and "S3," which excelled in special missions

As the purpose of the project became clearer, I ended up turning to other chips as well, depending on the application.

### The “First-Generation ESP32 (WROOM-32),” Which Was Indispensable for Car Data Loggers
This is the so-called original, “plain” ESP32. While the currently mainstream C and S series are dedicated to Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), only this first-generation WROOM supports the legacy **Bluetooth Classic (SPP, etc.)**. When I built a DIY logger that communicates wirelessly with an in-vehicle OBD2 adapter to retrieve vehicle logs, this Bluetooth Classic support was essential, so I had no other choice. As long as there’s a need for this application, the first-generation model remains indispensable to me.

### The "ESP32-S3" installed for PC operation
Previously, when I conducted an experiment on voice-controlled sleep and wake-up functions for a PC without soldering, I chose the ESP32-S3, which features native USB functionality. For applications where the device needs to be recognized by a PC as a standard USB keyboard or mouse (HID interface), the S3—with its built-in USB controller—is the clear choice.

## And that’s how we ended up with the nimble and reliable “C3”

After trying out all those detours and specialized applications, the **ESP32-C3** was the one that ended up staying by my side as my go-to companion for everyday electronics projects.

When I first got my hands on the C3 development board, I remember feeling a little unsure, thinking, “It’s so small it fits on my fingertip—will this really be enough?”

However, when I actually tried using it, both Wi-Fi and BLE worked exceptionally well, and the number of I/O pins for connecting the necessary sensors and LEDs was just right for a palm-sized project. For everyday IoT applications and basic sensor measurements, the C3’s single-core processor provided more than enough processing power.

What I liked most of all was **"its low power consumption and minimal heat generation."**

With high-clock-speed dual-core models, the circuit board gradually heats up just from running the device, which can make you a little worried about keeping it sealed in a case and running it continuously. In that regard, the C3 only gets slightly warm and doesn’t feel hot to the touch. This peace of mind that comes from the device “not getting hot” is a huge benefit for your peace of mind when using a gadget that’s left running continuously for long periods.

## Choosing Based on Purpose: My Guidelines for Selecting an ESP32

After trying out various options and comparing them, I now choose among them based on the specific use, as follows.

1. **"I haven't decided what I want to make yet, but I just want to have some fun for now."**
   → **I recommend inexpensive development boards with LCDs (such as the C6 or S3).** Just having a screen right in front of you makes it much easier to stay motivated over the long term.
2. **"I want to build my own USB device (HID) for my PC or run resource-intensive tasks on it"**
   → The **ESP32-S3** with native USB support.
3. **"I want to communicate with legacy Bluetooth devices, such as in-vehicle OBD2 systems."**
   → The **first-generation ESP32 (WROOM)**—the only option that supports Bluetooth Classic.
4. **"I don't have any particular preferences, but I want to build sensor-based projects and simple automations for everyday life."**
   → Without a doubt, the **ESP32-C3**.

Even if you want to connect four or more LEDs, switches, or sensors, you’ll have no trouble as long as you choose the C3 evaluation board, which has GPIO pins clearly exposed.

It’s small, affordable, generates little heat, and reliably handles all the tasks I need it to. This sense of it being a tool I can use without any fuss is why I’ve chosen the C3 as my absolute “favorite.”
