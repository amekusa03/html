# How a Friend’s Simple Comment—“I want to make my model kit light up”—Led to an ESP32 with 15 Lighting Patterns and a Web Remote Control

2026-10-15

## background

One day, a friend who loves model kits asked me, “I’d like to try making my plastic models light up with LEDs.” It wasn’t so much a request for advice as it was half a project proposal.

But bringing that up with an engineer who loves electronics projects was my downfall.

If I'm going to make it light up anyway, just having it stay on is no fun. I want it to flicker slowly, as if the power core were breathing; to shimmer as if the thrusters were spewing fire; and to flash faster and faster, like a warp charge, until it bursts into a flash of light.

Plus, if I put a physical switch inside the model kit, it seems like the tiny parts might break every time I press it. So, I’ll set it up so I can control it via Wi-Fi from my smartphone. If I can control it through a browser, I won’t need a dedicated app. Since it’s a hassle to share URLs or IP addresses, I want the screen to open automatically once connected. That way, I can add more lighting patterns and adjust the brightness individually…

...And just like that, my desire became unstoppable. My engineer's spirit—which I call my creative drive—had been completely ignited.

I have an ultra-compact, powerful microcontroller—the “ESP32-C6”—lying around here. It supports the latest Wi-Fi 6 standard, and its high-precision hardware PWM control (LEDC) lets you do whatever you want.

"I'll build you the best lighting system."

With that in mind, I dove into development over the weekend. As a result, I ended up with an “Illumination Studio” that was utterly over-engineered—far exceeding a simple LED lighting circuit, it was packed with **a smartphone web remote, a captive portal, over 15 types of sci-fi and mecha lighting effects, and an auto-save feature**.

## 4 Times I Went Too Far With My Obsessions

### 1. A Captive Portal-Style Web Remote That Eliminates the Need to Ask, “What’s a URL?”

If I ask a non-engineer friend to "open a browser and type `192.168.4.1` into the address bar," they won't be able to use it anymore at that point.

So, I configured the ESP32 to function as a Wi-Fi access point (SoftAP), set up a simple DNS server (`dns_server.c`), and implemented a “captive portal.”

When you select this network in your smartphone’s Wi-Fi settings, a browser opens automatically and a control screen appears—just like the connection screens for free Wi-Fi at cafes or in hotels. There’s no need to install an app or manually enter a URL. The moment you connect, a dark-theme console screen appears right on your device.

### 2. Over 15 lighting patterns packed with romance

I wrote the lighting animation in C so that the plastic model kit can accommodate mechs from any setting (`patterns.c`).

- **Spaceships and Mechs**
  - **Power Reactor Breath**: A sine wave was subjected to gamma correction to create a flickering effect that appears to the human eye like a natural deep breath.
  - **Thruster Firing**: I added random noise from `esp_random()` to create the effect of the vernier's exhaust gas flickering.
  - **Search Scanner**: Like in *Knight Rider*, a light beam moves back and forth from left to right.
  - **Warp Charge**: The flashing gradually speeds up, then emits a flash at its peak before entering a cooldown.
- **Aircraft and Scale Models**
  - **Navigation Lights & Strobe**: The position lights remain dimly lit, while the strobe flashes with a sharp double pulse.
  - **Landing Lights**: Just like during a landing approach, they gradually brighten to their maximum intensity.
- **Automotive and Special Effects**
  - **Starting the Engine & Idling**: It starts with a whirring cranking sound, then sputters to life, and transitions to a slightly shaky idle.
  - **Welding Sparks**: Irregular flashes that look like sparks crackling and flying. Perfect for a maintenance dock diorama.
  - **Intermittent Contact Noise**: The voltage occasionally drops, causing flickering—it has the feel of a battle-scarred, damaged unit.
  - **Firefly Synchronization**: Four LEDs glow organically, each with a slightly different phase.

### 3. 4-channel individual manual dimming to accommodate "We haven't decided which areas to illuminate yet"

My friend builds all kinds of model kits, but he says he hasn’t decided yet what kind of look he wants to give each model. Since there are so many possibilities, he needs a system that allows him to make changes later on.

Therefore, we assigned an independent hardware PWM (LEDC, 5 kHz) to each of the four channels (GPIO 0–3) and provided a dedicated dimming slider (0–100%) for each channel on the web interface.

You can fine-tune the lighting balance—such as “cockpit instrument panel dim (15%),” “main camera bright (60%),” and “rear thrusters fully lit (100%)”—using a single slider on your smartphone while looking at a physical model. It’s also very useful for checking wiring and LED polarity.

### 4. NVS Auto-Save: Never Forget Your Work, Even When You Turn Off the Power

It would be a real letdown to have to reset the lighting settings on my smartphone every time I turn it on after mounting it on a model kit stand or in a display case.

So, I set it up so that when you select a pattern, brightness, or speed on the web interface, the settings are automatically saved to the ESP32's built-in flash memory (NVS) a few seconds later.

Whether at a trade show or on a display shelf at home, just turn it on, and it will light up using your favorite lighting setting from the last time.
