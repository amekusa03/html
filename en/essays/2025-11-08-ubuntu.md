# I started Ubuntu

2026-11-08

## end of support for windows 10

Support for Windows 10 ended a while ago. My living room PC is a 4th generation Core i5, and no matter how hard I try, I can't get it to Windows 11. Motherboard, CPU, memory... I've been thinking about replacing it many times in my head, but I just can't seem to get around to it.

The reason is simple: in recent years, the opportunities to use PCs have been decreasing. If I want to do some quick research, my smartphone is enough, and I don't have the energy to get up from the sofa in the living room and go to my PC. Honestly, I didn't have the motivation to spend more to improve my environment.

## To Ubuntu

After thinking about it, the conclusion I came to was surprisingly simple. "Free is best." So I decided to adopt Ubuntu.

I was a little worried about not being able to use Windows apps. Letting go of an environment you've used for many years has more psychological hurdles than you might think. However, if you weigh the cost-effectiveness, the answer will be determined by itself. Once you are satisfied with the logic, all that is left is to take action. I decided to give it a try anyway.

## install

The steps are pretty normal. First, I backed up the entire boot SSD to a spare HDD. Next, create an Ubuntu installation USB, boot from it, and proceed with the installation.

The installation was completed in a blink of an eye. Next, I will install LibreOffice, VS Code, Chrome, etc. Perhaps because it has 12GB of memory, booting is quick and comfortable. There are no particular problems with the screen display, keyboard input, or audio.

The only stumbling block was the printer. Whenever I try to print, an error occurs. I decided to tackle this more slowly at a later date, and ended the day's work here. I'm also starting to feel a little sleepy.

## conclusion

If I had to sum up my thoughts from the day in one word, it would be "Ubuntu is free and amazing." That's all.

Now that I think about it, I have some experience working with Linux-based environments, including a Solaris workstation, a PC with Vine installed, and a microcomputer board with Debian installed. I think that's why this transition to Ubuntu didn't feel so difficult. It seems that getting used to it comes into play in situations like this.
