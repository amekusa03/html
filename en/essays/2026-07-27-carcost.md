# Comprehensive comparison and maintenance cost analysis of electric cars and gasoline cars

## 2026-07-27

In the automobile market as of 2026, the economics of choosing between an electric vehicle (EV) or a gasoline vehicle will be largely determined by mileage, charging environment, and the latest government subsidy system.

* Economic efficiency (TCO): The total cost of ownership (TCO) for 10 years is estimated to be approximately 650,000 yen less than a gasoline-powered vehicle. The break-even point will be reached in about 4.8 to 5 years, assuming the vehicle is driven 10,000 km per year and charged at home.
* Reform of the subsidy system: From January 2026, the CEV subsidy will be significantly reviewed, and the upper limit of EV will be increased from the current 900,000 yen to 1.3 million yen. On the other hand, prices for fuel cell vehicles (FCVs) will be reduced, correcting the price difference with EVs.
* Vehicle characteristics and maintenance: EVs are 300 to 400 kg heavier than gasoline-powered vehicles in the same class due to their batteries, which puts a greater burden on tires and suspension. On the other hand, regular maintenance costs are significantly reduced because engine-related parts are not required.
* Environmental impact: Although EVs emit more emissions during the manufacturing stage, they are expected to reduce CO2 emissions by approximately 33% over the entire life cycle (when driven 200,000 km) than gasoline vehicles.
* Resale value: The residual value rate of EVs still tends to be lower (around 25-36%) compared to gasoline cars (around 40% for popular models), but on a ``real cost'' basis, taking into account subsidies, it is becoming comparable to gasoline cars.

## Economics and cost of ownership (TCO) analysis

### 10 year total cost of ownership comparison

According to the latest calculations, the total cost over 10 years, including vehicle purchase price, maintenance costs, taxes, fuel and electricity costs, is as follows.

| Item | Electric vehicle (EV) | Gasoline vehicle
| - | - | -
| Total cost for 10 years | Approx. 5.92 million yen | Approx. 6.58 million yen
| Fuel/energy cost (per 1km) | Approx. 5 yen | Approx. 8.8 yen

* Fuel cost advantage: Assuming gasoline price of 175.2 yen/L and electricity cost of 30 yen/kWh, EV will save approximately 38,000 yen over 10,000 km per year.
* Break-even point: When subsidies are used (CEV subsidies, etc.), the time it takes to recover the difference in purchase price through reduced fuel and maintenance costs is approximately 4.8 to 5 years under standard operating conditions.

## Latest trends in subsidies and taxation from 2026

### Review of CEV subsidy (from January 2026)

The subsidy amount will change significantly from 2026 due to the background of Japan-U.S. tariff negotiations.

* EV (regular car): Maximum 900,000 yen → Increased to maximum 1,300,000 yen.
* PHV (Plug-in Hybrid): Increased from maximum 600,000 yen to maximum 850,000 yen.
* Light EV: 580,000 yen (fixed).
* FCV (fuel cell vehicle): Maximum 2.55 million yen → significantly reduced to 1.5 million yen.

### Trends in tax benefits and tax increases

* Greening special provisions (vehicle tax reduction/exemption): Extended until the end of March 2028.
* Eco-car tax reduction (weight tax): Tax exemption for EVs, etc. will continue until the second vehicle inspection.
* Consideration of taxation: The government is considering raising taxes on EVs and PHVs starting in May 2028 (including discussions on mileage tax, etc.) due to the ``burden that heavy batteries place on roads,'' and it has been pointed out that there is a contradiction between promoting widespread use and tightening taxes.

### Differences in vehicle structure and maintenance

### Impact of increased weight

Because EVs are equipped with large-capacity lithium-ion batteries (200 to 500 kg) to ensure their cruising range, they are on average 300 to 400 kg heavier than gasoline-powered vehicles.

* Weight comparison example:
  * Honda e (1,540kg) vs Fit (1,140kg): difference 400kg
  * bZ4X (1,960kg) vs RAV4 (1,570kg): Difference 390kg
* Advantages: The battery is placed under the floor, lowering the center of gravity and improving driving and straight-line stability.
* Disadvantages: Increased stress on tires, suspension, and brakes, and tires tend to wear out more quickly than gasoline-powered cars.

### Differences in maintenance items

EVs have fewer moving parts and do not require engine-related maintenance, so long-term maintenance costs can be kept low.

* Items not required for EV: Engine oil change, air filter change, spark plug change, transmission oil change.
* Points to note: It is important to manage battery deterioration (SOH) and regularly check tire air pressure as weight increases.

## Environmental impact and energy efficiency

### Life cycle CO2 emissions

* Manufacturing stage: Due to the large energy consumption during battery manufacturing, EVs have a higher environmental impact immediately after manufacturing.
* Driving/supply stage: In addition to zero exhaust emissions, as the proportion of renewable energy increases, the emission reduction effect will increase.
* Overall evaluation: When driving 200,000 km, cumulative emissions can be reduced by approximately 33% compared to gasoline vehicles.

### energy conversion efficiency

* EV: Conversion efficiency from electricity to power is approximately 90%.
* Gasoline vehicles: Heat loss during combustion is large, and efficiency remains at around 30%.

## Current status and future forecast of resale value (asset value)

### Comparison of residual value setting credits (residual value rate)

In terms of residual value based on the expected trade-in value after five years, EVs are at a slightly lower level than gasoline cars.

| Vehicle classification | 5-year residual value rate guideline
| - | -
| Gasoline vehicle (popular model) | 40% to 60%
| Main EV models | 25% to 36%

* Examples of major EVs: Nissan Leaf (approx. 36%), Tesla Model Y (approx. 35%), Toyota bZ4X (31%), Suzuki e-Vitara (27%).

### Positive factors that affect asset value

* Value based on actual cost: Calculating the "real purchase price" after subtracting subsidies, the residual value of the bZ4X reaches approximately 43% (approximately 50% with subsidies added if you live in Tokyo), which is comparable to a gasoline-powered car.
* Visualization of SOH (state of health): Transparency in the used market is improving due to battery diagnostic certificates provided by third-party organizations and trials of the "battery condition certificate" initiated by Nissan.
* Secondary use of batteries: Establishing reuse technology to reuse used batteries as stationary storage batteries will support the residual value of vehicles.

## Conclusion: Decision criteria for implementation

The conditions for maximizing the economic benefits of EVs are as follows.

* Availability of home charging environment: Quick charging on the go can cost 2 to 3 times as much as charging at home, so home charging is a prerequisite for economic efficiency.
* Mileage: If you drive more than 12,000 to 15,000 km per year, you can quickly recover the difference in vehicle price, and the advantage of EVs becomes decisive.
* Ownership period: The TCO reduction effect is significant if the battery is operated within the scope of the battery warranty (mostly 8 years and 160,000 km) or if it is assumed to be owned for a long period of 10 years or more.
* Utilizing the latest subsidies: It is recommended that you choose the timing of your purchase, taking into account the increase (up to 1.3 million yen) from January 2026.
