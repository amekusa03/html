# Smart Just by Plugging It In? — My Attempt to Control a PC Power Supply Without Soldering Using the ESP32-S3 —

2026-11-03

## Introduction: Taking on the Challenge Without Additional Parts or Solder

I want to control my PC using just my voice by saying, “OK Google, turn on the computer” or “Turn off the computer” to my smart speaker.

A while back, I built a system—which I also wrote about on my blog—that involved inserting a micro-relay into the motherboard’s power pin (PWR SW) and using a photocoupler to monitor the power supply. While that method certainly allows you to turn the power on and off reliably, it requires the extra effort of opening the case and routing the wires, as well as additional components like a relay.

So, the theme for this project is **“No soldering, zero additional parts”**.
It’s a challenge to see just how smart we can make it by simply plugging an ESP32-S3 board into the USB port on the back of a PC.

## How It Works: The Advantages of Standard USB HID

Since I didn't want to install any dedicated software or resident agents on the PC, I configured the ESP32-S3 to function as a standard "USB keyboard" as defined by the operating system.

- **Power Off (Sleep)**: Send the Consumer Control sleep key code (`0x0032`) via USB
- **Power On (Resume)**: Sends a USB Remote Wake-up signal and a Space key press
- **Status Detection**: Directly monitors USB bus communication (suspended state) and reflects it on Google Home with zero delay

There are absolutely no external components, such as relays or optocouplers. Since it functions as a standard HID device, **it worked exactly the same way on both Windows and Linux (Ubuntu) without requiring any additional drivers or dedicated applications**. A major advantage of this approach is that it works immediately upon being plugged into a USB port, regardless of the OS environment.

## Result: The Line Between Success and Failure

After actually putting it together and testing it, the result was **"half a success, half a failure"**.

- **What it can do**: Entering and waking from sleep mode. Just say the word, and the screen dims and the PC goes to sleep; say it again, and it wakes up instantly. Synchronization while running is also flawless.
- **What didn't work**: Turning the power on from a completely powered-off state (shutdown).

When the PC enters a full shutdown (S5), power to the USB ports is cut off due to the motherboard's specifications, causing the ESP32 itself to go silent. Even if external power were supplied, there were significant barriers—such as the BIOS and hardware—preventing a cold boot via USB.

## summary

I know that with the old method of connecting a relay to the motherboard, you can control the system all the way up to a full power-on, but the sheer “convenience” of being able to control sleep mode simply by plugging a single board into a USB port—without any soldering or additional components—is truly overwhelming.

If you’re willing to accept “sleep mode” as the norm with a modern PC, this setup is more than comfortable enough for practical use. It was a challenge that let me experience the fun of building a system with no modifications or additional parts, while exploring just how “smart” it could be while prioritizing convenience.
