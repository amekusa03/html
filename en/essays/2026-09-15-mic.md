# Cut out the noise and let only your voice be heard

2026-09-15 / Python / DSP / Audio

## chance

When I'm on a remote call, I've started to notice that the sound of the air conditioner and the noise from my computer's fan are picking up on the microphone.

When I tried out some off-the-shelf noise-canceling tools, my voice ended up sounding unnaturally muffled. The heavy processing—which I’d describe as a “robotic voice”—actually seemed to strip the conversation of its warmth. The configurable parameters were a black box; I couldn’t see which frequency bands were being cut or how.

"Well then, I'll just make it myself," I thought.

## PureVoice

A real-time audio processing tool written in Python specifically for microphones.

![GUI](essays/2026-09-15-mic.png)

By using `sounddevice`, we achieved low latency with callback processing at block sizes ranging from 480 to 960 (10 to 20 ms). The DSP is configured in a three-tier architecture.

First, use a **high-pass filter** to cut out the muffled low-frequency noise below 80 Hz. Next, use a **noise gate** to detect the slight white noise present during silent moments, and reduce it while smoothly adjusting the attack and release times. Finally, use the **peak limiter and gain compensation** to prevent sudden clipping while boosting the overall volume so that even a whisper can be heard by the other person.

The UI was built using PySide6 (Qt), and the GUI thread was decoupled from the audio callbacks. Even when changing settings while dragging, the audio never cuts out, not even for a moment.

## Give it a try

It doesn't have any flashy features, but it gives you a real sense of "hands-on" control. Every time you move a slider, you can hear exactly how each frequency band changes.

Polishing your voice using only the materials you have on hand offers a quiet, understated joy, much like tuning the strings of a musical instrument.
