# slow farming method

## 2026-07-26

## my field

My farm is in the mountains.

Even though it's a field, there's a large lawn, so when we arrived, the first thing we did was spread out the chairs and tables before we started working. This is our style. Before touching the soil, let's start with some tea and a chat. Today, I sat down and relaxed, saying things like, "It's 70% hot, isn't it?"

## crops

Now, when I looked at the field, the lettuce was in full bloom. It's already past the best time to eat it, but things like this actually make it more fun. I'm secretly planning to collect some seeds.

On the other hand, eggplants, tomatoes, and watermelons have been destroyed by pests and have almost been destroyed. I let out a sigh. Every year, I have high hopes that this year will happen, but I always crumble in front of their (beasts) appetite.

I tried mowing the grass a little and found myself sweating in no time. The one with physical strength made a sound first and immediately gave up. Then I'll have lunch and then go back to tea time. Once I had a snack, I felt a sense of accomplishment and fullness at the same time, and I headed home feeling somewhat satisfied. I think this is also one way to spend time in the field.

## desire

However, to be honest, I still want freshly harvested vegetables. Isn't there a convenient crop that requires less effort and is less likely to be targeted by pests?

Looking back at what I have researched on the internet and put into practice, this is what it looks like.

- Lettuce: It won't grow big, but it won't be inedible.
- Pumpkin: Flowers bloom, but as soon as the fruit ripens, it is eaten by birds.
- Watermelon: Killed by birds
- Eggplant: Prey for pests
- Potatoes: For some reason they don't grow
- Zucchini: There are years when it doesn't grow.
- Peanuts: There are many years when they do not grow.
- Green pepper: This also doesn't get big.
- Mikan: Trees don't grow in the first place.
- Turnip: sprouts but does not grow large
- Daikon: This is just a good job.
- Silk pod and pea: Only occasionally attacked by pests.

When you line them up like this, you can clearly see that things are not going very well. Still, I find myself trying to plant something again next year without giving up. This may also be a way of interacting with mountain fields.
