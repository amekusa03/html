# Rhythmbox crashes after upgrading to Ubuntu 26.10

2026-10-11

## background

When you upgrade your operating system, apps you use regularly may suddenly stop working. After upgrading to Ubuntu 26, my music player, Rhythmbox, started crashing during playback. Since the cause turned out to be in an unexpected place, I’m documenting the steps I took to investigate it.

## Symptoms

The symptoms were clear. Whenever I played a song, a notification would appear every time the track changed. A few seconds later, the entire window would disappear. There was no error dialog—it just vanished.

At first, I had no idea what was wrong. So I recorded the screen to check how it behaved. A song would change, a notification would appear, and the window would disappear. This sequence was always the same. The fact that it was reproducible was a big help in my investigation.

## Read the log

I launched Rhythmbox from the terminal to check the crash message.

```
Rhythmbox:ERROR:../backends/gstreamer/rb-player-gst-multi.c:1251:impl_play: assertion failed: (current != NULL)
```

`impl_play` is a function that starts playback, and it assumes that “the stream currently being played exists.” When that assumption was violated, the app would crash on its own. This means that something failed on the playback side, and the app proceeded to the next step while the playback target remained empty.

## Clues

When I searched for the same error, I found a similar report for Fedora 44. It stated that **the problem was resolved by disabling the ReplayGain plugin**.

ReplayGain is a feature designed to even out volume differences between tracks. Since it works by inserting GStreamer elements during playback, it is susceptible to changes made on the GStreamer side. It is possible that an OS upgrade, which replaced the dependent libraries, triggered the issue.

## solution

It was a simple fix. I opened the Rhythmbox plugin settings and unchecked the "ReplayGain" box. Just doing that was enough to stop the program from crashing when I switched tracks.

There was no need for extensive debugging. I just read the logs, looked for reports of the same issue, and disabled one suspicious plugin. That was all it took.

## Things I Noticed

- **Capture the log first.** I understood the behavior from the video, but I figured out the cause from the error message in the terminal.
- **It's worth questioning the plugin.** It's faster to isolate the features added later rather than the main app itself.
- **It's often not just you who experiences issues right after an upgrade.** If you look for reports of the same problem, you'll find a shortcut.

Since I turned off ReplayGain, there might be differences in volume between songs. If an update to Ubuntu fixes this, I can just turn it back on and check again.

It seems we need to pay a little attention to the maintenance status of Rhythmbox itself. Since it's an app I've been using for a long time, I'd like to keep an eye on its future developments.
