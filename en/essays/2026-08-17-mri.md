# What I thought inside the donut -- My first MRI experience

I had an MRI scan today.
The reason is not a big deal. I recently had some symptoms that I was concerned about, and when I consulted my family doctor, he said, ``I'd like to take some pictures just in case,'' and an appointment was scheduled. There is no word that stirs my imagination as much as the word "just in case." It's just my nature to find myself in the waiting room, clutching my smartphone, typing into the search box, ``Are there any differences between manufacturers?''

As I researched it, I discovered that it was a much deeper world than I had expected.

## There are many types of donuts

When I thought of MRI, all I could think of was my head being sucked into that cylindrical machine. In fact, that's exactly what I entered today, and as someone who is claustrophobic myself, I was honestly quite prepared.

However, upon investigation, it appears that the ``cylindrical shape'' is just one aspect of the technology in which the world's major manufacturers are competing. Overseas companies such as Siemens, GE, and Philips are competing with each other by focusing on image quality and imaging speed. In particular, Siemens is said to be able to achieve both high-definition images and time savings through AI-based image reconstruction, so it's no wonder that it is so useful at university hospitals and research institutes. GE uses an ``AIR coil,'' a coil that is as soft as a blanket, and seems to be aiming to reduce the burden on the body. Philips seems to even have a device called ``Ambient Experience'' that creates a relaxing space in the examination room itself with images and lighting. I think I'll look for a facility like that the next time I have an MRI.

Domestic manufacturers, on the other hand, are taking a different approach. As the name ``Pianissimo'' suggests, Canon Medical Systems sells its silent technology that thoroughly suppresses impact sounds similar to those found at construction sites, and aims to achieve high image quality even in low magnetic fields through noise reduction using AI. Fujifilm Healthcare is even more interesting and says it is a pioneer in "open MRI," which does not fit into a tube. It is a type that can be inserted from the top and bottom, making it suitable for people with claustrophobia, children, and the elderly. If I had gone to a Fujifilm facility today, I might not have felt that unique feeling of pressure.

## The day I learned that silence is justice

What surprised me the most when I actually took the test was that "sound." Although I had been told in advance that the intermittent banging sound would be loud, it was louder than I expected. It felt like someone was doing construction right next to my ears, and the music playing through the headphones I was provided with didn't make much sense.

From this experience, the story of Canon's silent technology called ``Pianissimo'' really stuck with me. If that sound can be significantly suppressed, that alone should lower the psychological hurdles for testing. Because of my job as an engineer, I tended to focus on performance and precision, but when I lay there as a patient, I realized that ``quietness'' is the most tangible value. I feel like I was reminded once again that the essence of technology is something that cannot be measured just by the numbers on a spec sheet.

## The difference between manufacturers is ultimately "who is it designed for?"

What I found interesting in this research is that the strengths of each company are not simply a performance competition. Overseas companies are competing in different arenas, with image quality, research, and cutting-edge technology as the core, while domestic manufacturers are competing in quietness, ease of installation, and generous support. It is interesting that it is not just the machine that determines the image quality, as it is said that not only the physical specifications such as whether the magnetic field strength is 1.5T or 3.0T, but also the skill of the technician in charge of the inspection influences the image quality.

After completing the test, I got curious and looked at the hospital's website, and found something unexpected. This hospital had three MRI machines, all made by Siemens.

* MAGNETOM Avanto Fit
* MAGNETOM Avanto Dot
* MAGNETOM Vida

Although bearing the same name as the "MAGNETOM" series, the introduction period is a total of 16 years until 2024. The latest Vida is probably loaded with cutting-edge AI technology like the aforementioned Deep Resolve, while the Avantos are veterans who continue to be active despite repeated improvements.

The problem is that I have no idea which aircraft I'm in today. When I looked back at the medical record, I didn't even have the model name written down, and I didn't have time to ask about it during the examination. Next time I have a chance to take one, I think I'll ask the technician in charge, ``Is this Vida?'' to ease my fears. You might think I'm an unusual patient.

## Conclusion

There were no abnormalities at all, and I fell asleep despite the loud noise. It may be an occupational disease for engineers that the more anxious they are, the more they investigate unrelated matters. On the other hand, some of me seem to be able to get a good night's sleep only during those times.

The next time I have the opportunity to undergo an MRI, I would definitely like to use a machine from a manufacturer that prides itself on its silent technology.
