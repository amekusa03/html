# How to read speaker catalog specifications

## 2026-07-25

Organize the numbers and terms listed in speaker catalogs and product pages so that they can be used in the actual selection process.

## 1. Basic specification items

### Model (method)

- **2-way / 3-way**: How many units will share the bass to treble? The greater the number of ways, the more each band can be played by a dedicated unit, but the price and size also increase.
- **Bookshelf type/Tallboy type**: Classification of installation style. Bookshelf is a small size that can be placed on a shelf or stand, while tallboy is a vertical type that can be placed on the floor.

### Playback frequency band (f special)

- Example: Written as "45Hz ~ 60kHz"
- **The smaller the lower limit value, the better the bass extension** This is a guideline (however, if the output level is not stated, it is only for reference)
- The upper limit often exceeds the human audible range (~20kHz), and the practical difference is small.
- Catalog values ​​may differ depending on the product, such as "-10dB", so be careful when comparing side-by-side with other models.

### Impedance (nominal impedance)

- The unit is Ω (ohm). Most are **4Ω** or **6Ω / 8Ω**
- It is necessary to match the corresponding impedance on the amplifier side. Connecting speakers with a value lower than the minimum supported impedance of the amplifier may place too much load on the amplifier.
- The lower the value, the easier it is to drive, but the load on the amplifier tends to be larger.

### Output sound pressure level (efficiency/sensitivity)

- Unit is **dB/W (1m)**. "Sound pressure when 1W input is measured at 1m point"
- The higher the number, the louder the sound produced with less amplifier output = the better the efficiency**
- Approximately: 88dB or less = low efficiency, 89 to 91dB = standard, 92dB or more = high efficiency
- Low-efficiency speakers require more power from the amplifier to produce the same volume.

### Maximum input/rated input

- **Rated input**: Guideline value that is safe to input continuously (W)
- **Maximum input (momentary maximum)**: Value that can be endured for only a moment. Although the large numbers stand out in the catalog, the rated input is more useful in practice.
- Check that the amplifier's output does not significantly exceed the speaker's rated input.

### crossover frequency

- Boundary frequency for switching between bass and treble units on a multiway device
- Example: If it is "3kHz", the woofer is responsible for the lower frequency band, and the tweeter is responsible for the higher frequency band.

### Unit configuration (caliber)

- Diameter of each diaphragm (cm/mm) for woofer (bass), midrange (midrange), and tweeter (treble)
- In general, the larger the aperture, the easier it is to produce a sense of bass volume, but there is a trade-off with installation space.

## 2. Guidelines for priorities to consider when making selections

1. **Is the impedance compatible with the amplifier** (required condition)
2. Balance between **output sound pressure level** and amplifier output (W) — If the amplifier has a reasonable output, it can be driven even at low efficiency.
3. **Lower limit of playback frequency band** — important if bass is important, lower priority if conversation is the main purpose (movie lines, etc.)
4. **Size/Installation method** — Does it physically fit in the installation location?

## 3. Points to note

- Catalog specs often have measurement conditions that are not disclosed or unified, so even if the numbers are the same, the actual hearing sensation may not necessarily match.
- Rather than comparing only numerical values, it is practical to narrow down priorities according to purpose (mainly music or mainly movies).
- Comparisons within the same manufacturer and same series often have the same measurement conditions and are easy to use as a reference.
