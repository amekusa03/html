# The Car Audio Rabbit Hole, Part 10: "Aren't External Amplifiers Important?"

2026-11-25

## "Of course, it's super important!" ...I'd like to say that, but

Now that we’ve covered speakers, sound deadening, subwoofers, DSPs, and various cables, any audio enthusiast might be thinking:
“Wait a minute—what about the ‘power amplifier,’ the heart of any audio system? Isn’t the amp important?”

Let me get straight to the point.
**Of course, it’s incredibly important.**

When you replace your speakers and fine-tune the sound using a DSP, you may suddenly hit a wall where you feel that “the sound is thin” or “it somehow lacks power.” This is often not the fault of the speakers, but rather due to the limited power of the amplifier built into the navigation system or head unit (limitations in power supply or output).
Introducing a standalone “external amplifier (power amplifier)” here will take the sharpness of the sound’s attack, the impact of the low frequencies, and the headroom when you turn up the volume to a whole new level.

"So, everyone, you should definitely get an external amplifier!"
...That's what I'd love to shout from the rooftops, but there's one thing I have to admit here, even if it means swallowing my pride.

**My current car doesn't have an external amplifier.**

## The Tragedy of a Man Whose Life Was Taken Over by a Subwoofer

I can almost hear someone saying, “You’ve been spouting all this knowledge to everyone, but you’re not even wearing it yourself?!”—but there’s a heartfelt reason behind this that I can’t talk about without tearing up.

There's only one reason: **"There's simply no more space left in the car for an amplifier."**

As I mentioned in Part 6, my beloved car is a Honda with virtually no space under the front seats.
Under the ironclad rule of not sacrificing interior comfort or cargo capacity, the precious prime real estate I barely managed to carve out under the rear seats has already been completely taken over by the subwoofer I added.

A “subwoofer” or an “external amplifier”?
Faced with this ultimate dilemma due to limited space inside the car, and desperate for a solid bass foundation, I reluctantly chose the subwoofer.
When I see people who’ve neatly tucked an external amplifier under their seats or in the trunk floor, I feel so envious I could bite my handkerchief in frustration.

## I can't forget "that sound" I experienced in my previous car.

I’d be upset if you thought, “So you’re just making things up even though you’ve never actually used one,” so please let me offer a brief explanation.
Actually, in the car I owned before this one, I did install an external amplifier. That’s exactly why I know firsthand just how incredible the effect is.

The world of amplifiers ranges from the very best to the very worst, but there’s no need to buy a super-high-end model costing hundreds of thousands of yen. Even entry-level models retailing for tens of thousands of yen, or used, discontinued models found on online auctions, offer a world of difference compared to the amplifiers built into car navigation systems.
The driving power that precisely controls the speaker cones at will, and the vivid realism that makes it seem as though you can hear even the singer’s every breath—these are the overwhelming advantages unique to a separate amplifier.

## If there's room, just "Front 2ch" will do.

If there's still some space under the seats of your beloved car and your budget allows it, adding an external amplifier is definitely a step I'd strongly recommend.

There’s no need to suddenly set up amplifiers for all four channels and do a major wiring job. Just by powering the “two front speakers”—the heart of the music—with an external amplifier, the soundstage right in front of you will transform into something remarkably powerful.
Thanks to recent technological advances, there are now many ultra-compact, high-efficiency digital amplifiers (Class-D) available that can be hidden deep inside the glove compartment or behind the center console.

The romance of pursuing the ideal sound versus the harsh reality of limited space inside the car.
Struggling, improvising, and sometimes compromising as you navigate that tightrope to create your own unique sound—that may be the true joy of DIY car audio.

……So, is there still room in your car to squeeze in an amp?
