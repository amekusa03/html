# It might be better not to plant loquats in the garden.

## 2026.07.19 / Daily life

## It's a waste not to eat it

Sometimes I see loquat trees planted there.
When the time comes to eat, I look up at the tree,
It has many fruits.

But no one picks them, so they just rot.
It's such a waste.
The loquat trees on my farm are harvested while competing with pests.
(There are also years when we lose completely.)

## Fruit thinning and growth

If you thin the loquat just a little, the fruit will grow larger.
Even if you just remove a few small berries from the bunch with your fingers, the size of the remaining berries will noticeably change.
It doesn't take much effort, so there's no reason not to do it.

## The price of pruning

Here's the problem. The growth rate is unusually fast.
A few years ago, I left a seedling that was about 30cm long, and before I knew it, it had grown to over 2m.
If you don't prune it every year, the fruit will quickly grow out of reach.
Pruning shears won't cut it, so you'll have to bring out a saw.
If it weren't for this pruning, I would definitely recommend this tree.

## Byproduct of pruning, loquat tea

However, pruning can also help.
Selecting beautiful leaves from the cut branches, drying them for several weeks and brewing them makes delicious tea.
(Members love it)

## How to make my own loquat tea

- Use about 5 or 6 leaves at a time (the more the better)
- Dry in the shade for 3 to 4 weeks
- Once dry, cut into 2cm pieces with scissors.
- Put it in a pot or kettle and simmer for about 30 minutes.

## In the end, should I plant it?

Although it has such useful value, it is also a tree that I have to fight with with a saw every year.
If you don't mind pruning or even enjoy pruning, you may want to plant it in your garden.
If not, I honestly don't recommend it.
