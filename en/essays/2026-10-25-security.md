# Security Risk Management

## 2026-10-26 / IT

## These days

I wrote an essay about PHP the other day, but this time I’d like to discuss measures and awareness regarding security risk management.

I started by briefly reviewing the situation regarding a certain service that had suffered a security breach.

My impression was that the system was being operated without adequate basic security measures in place, and I felt it was as risky as exposing a PC without antivirus software to the Internet.
I suspect that the engineer who set up the system was somewhat aware of this situation.

So, why did this situation arise?
The first thing that comes to mind is corporate governance.
To what extent was management aware of this situation?
Based on the information that has been made public, I feel there is a possibility that they were not fully aware of it.
One clue to this is their response to the situation.
The fact that they offered condolence payments and compensation first may have been an attempt to bring the situation to a swift close.
As a service provider, isn’t it important to first conduct a prompt investigation into the cause and extent of the damage and provide an explanation?
Only then, based on the extent of the damage, should they have determined the details and amount of compensation.

There's one more thing that concerns me.
I wonder if some users of this service had already been aware of the security risks.

Although there were apparently calls for the Japanese government to take action, I believe the current response is sufficient for this specific incident.

If there is one thing the government should do, it is to establish a legal framework.
For example, I believe a situation like this could have been prevented if there had been some rules in place for services that handle critical data.
Furthermore, when it comes to establishing rules, education is key.
I believe it is essential not only to have rules but also to provide education that helps people understand the gravity of the situation.
After all, there are times when simply saying “I didn’t know” is not an acceptable excuse.
