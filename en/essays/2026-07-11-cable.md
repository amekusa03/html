## 1. Wiring size is determined by "A (current)" rather than "W (power)"

The cause of heat generation in wires is Joule heat (P = I²R), and the magnitude of the current itself is the criterion for determining wire size.
The number of watts is simply the result of multiplying voltage x current, so if the number of watts is the same but the voltage is different, the required current and gauge will change.

### Even with the same 150W, if the voltage changes, the current will change.

| Voltage system | Calculation | Current |
|---|---|---|
| 12V system (15V equivalent for practical use) | 150W ÷ 15V | 10A |
| 24V series | 150W ÷ 24V | 6.25A |
| 200V series (reference: 750W) | 750W ÷ 200V | 3.75A |

→ Since the voltage of the 12V system in cars is low, the current tends to be large even with the same wattage.

### Meaning of “1000W compatible” for household cords

100V x 10A = 1000W. For home use, the voltage (100V) is fixed, so there is no contradiction even if you convert it to W display.
The essence of the design remains the current (A) standard.

## 2. Conclusion (power cable: 10A・2m)

- **Required Gauge: 16AWG(1.25~1.31sq)OFC**
- There is no problem with voltage drop if it is 2m (less than 1% for 12V)
- Cables that are too thick such as 8AWG have over specs in terms of current capacity,
  Small terminals such as bullets cannot be crimped, resulting in non-compatibility.

## 3. Reasons for choosing OFC (oxygen-free copper)

- High conductivity and low voltage drop even with the same gauge
- More flexible and resistant to vibration and handling than CCA (copper clad aluminum)
- There are products that are labeled as "OFC" but are actually CCA core wire + OFC plating, so
  Check the material description and weight of the conductor (too light = possibility of aluminum contamination)

### Comparison summary of conductors

| Types of conductor | Ease of conducting electricity | Lightness/softness | Cost | Main uses |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| TPC | Standard | Standard | Low | General home appliances/wiring |
| OFC | High | Standard | Medium to High | Audio/AV equipment |
| CCA | Low (about 60% of copper) | Very light and soft | Very low | Cheap cable/in-vehicle |
| PC-Triple C | Highest | Standard | Extremely high | High-end audio |

## 5. Peripheral knowledge related to voltage and current

### Reasons why high-voltage power lines are thin

If you send the same amount of power (W), the higher the voltage, the smaller the current will be.

- Wires need to be thinner (reduced material costs)
- Transmission loss (P = I²R) is also reduced
As a trade-off, the higher the voltage, the more likely dielectric breakdown (corona discharge/arc discharge) will occur.
Countermeasures for high-voltage lines include insulators and insulation distances.

### Why is the CPU low voltage?

The main purpose is not to deal with discharge or insulation issues, but to suppress heat generation.

- Power consumption is proportional to P ∝ C・V²・f, so when the voltage is lowered, heat generation increases by the square of the voltage and is greatly reduced.
- Due to miniaturization of transistors, the internal insulating film (oxide film) becomes thinner, so there is a possibility that it will break if the voltage is not lowered.
- As a side benefit, the smaller the voltage swing, the faster the signal rises, which is advantageous for high clock operation.

## Supplement: Construction

### Giboshi

- **Do not connect silver and gold as much as possible**
  Aluminum may gradually corrode at the contact point (white powdery oxide is generated) and contact resistance may increase (catalytic corrosion of dissimilar metals).
- **Do not solder crimp**
  When the solder seeps into the core wire through capillary action, it becomes susceptible to breakage due to vibration, which can lead to failure.

### cable

- **Do not wrap excess cable in a circle**
  If you wrap it in a circle, it will become like an antenna and may pick up noise. Collect the remainder into a figure 8.
- **Route control/signal lines separately from power cables**
  If possible, do so at right angles of 90 degrees (to minimize noise induction).

## Summary: One law that sticks

> The size and design of wires and circuits are always determined by A (current), not W (power).
> If you increase the voltage, the current will decrease and the wiring can be made thinner.
> This relationship applies to automotive (12V), household (100V), industrial (200V/24V), power grid (high voltage),
> Even the semiconductor (CPU) is connected by the same principle (P = IV, P = I²R), just on a different scale.
