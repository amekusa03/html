# What kind of car is best for going outdoors?

2026-08-01

When choosing a car, people usually have two faces in mind. I go outdoors on holidays, and I go shopping at the supermarket on weekdays. The trouble is, these two want completely different cars.

## SUV

SUVs are reliable. The ground clearance is high, and with 4WD, it can handle even snowy ruts. Even if the tires get lost in a rut, they won't scratch the bottom of the car, and the traction can be distributed between the front and rear wheels, giving you a sense of security on snowy roads. Many car models come standard with roof rails, allowing luggage that does not fit inside the car to escape onto the roof. Being able to earn a payload of ``inside the car + on top of the car'' is quite strong.

## minivan

On the other hand, minivans are very popular. Fold the third row of seats to reveal a spacious, flat cargo area, with plenty of room even if you throw in a tent, tarp, table, chairs, and cooler box all at once. If you have a family of four or more, the loading capacity and ease of getting in and out (sliding doors, low floor) will be an overwhelming asset. This is especially true when children are young.

## compact car

And compact cars are a humble but honest option. It is easy to maneuver and can be used on narrow mountain roads and parking lots in residential areas without worrying. It has good fuel efficiency, and reduces vehicle price and maintenance costs. Thanks to VSC (side skid prevention system), skidding and spins on unpaved roads and muddy roads are now considerably suppressed. However, this only assumes that the tires can grip the road surface. If the tires get completely stuck in a deep rut and start spinning, no matter how good the VSC is, it won't solve the physical problem of not being able to move. This is the decisive difference between it and an SUV.

## Which one is better after all?

In the end, the correct answer cannot be determined just by looking at the car's spec sheet. The deciding factors are always four axes.

The first is the number of people. If you have a family of two and only occasionally go outdoors, you can get by in a compact car by folding down the rear seats. However, if four or more people are seriously loading luggage, the story changes.

The second factor is the frequency and seriousness of outdoor activities. If you only go out once or twice a month, a compact car is an overall good deal because it reduces the burden of everyday use. On the other hand, if you frequently travel to really bad roads or snowy areas, you will benefit greatly from an SUV or minivan, even if you sacrifice some of the usual fuel efficiency and maneuverability.

The third one is the specific gravity for everyday use. If your main purpose is commuting or shopping, and you only go outdoors once in a while, the fuel efficiency and ease of handling will be more beneficial in everyday situations.

The fourth factor is the presence or absence of bad roads and snow. This is the only area where compact cars and minivans can't break free from the SUV stronghold. The margin for rut depth and the traction of 4WD/AWD are two physical advantages created by the minimum ground clearance and drive system, and are areas that cannot be controlled by electronic control.

If you line them up like this, the hierarchy of vehicle price and running cost will also roughly follow this same line. Compact cars are the cheapest, have better fuel efficiency, and have lighter taxes. As minivans are equipped with more equipment, their vehicle prices and running costs tend to rise. SUVs are somewhere in between, but if you choose AWD or large tires, you'll quickly move towards the minivan side.

In other words, if you are a family of two and only go outdoors once in a while, and then go to work or go shopping the rest of the time, the answer is surprisingly simple. There is no need to force yourself to choose an SUV or minivan; a compact car will suffice. If you're concerned about rough roads or snow, you could look for a compact car with AWD, which is becoming increasingly popular these days.

In the end, choosing a car may be a matter of choosing which voice to listen to louder: your holiday self or your weekday self.
