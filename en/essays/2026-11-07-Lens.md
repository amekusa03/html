# The morning sun shines on the reading glasses on my desk — Near-misses Hidden in Everyday Life —

2026-11-07

## This Morning's Events

Around 8:00 this morning, as I headed to my computer desk as usual, a straight beam of light was streaming in through a tiny gap in the curtains.

Perhaps it’s because the seasons are shifting from fall to winter, and the sun’s angle has gradually grown lower. The morning sun is quietly reaching even the far corner of my desk—a spot that usually doesn’t get any sunlight.

As I happened to glance over, I noticed my reading glasses—propped up on their stand—glistening in the light. I was idly gazing at them, thinking, “They’re beautiful,” as part of an ordinary moment in my daily life, but as I followed the path of the light passing through the lenses, a chill ran down my spine.

## Focus Set on a Black Frame

The light passing through the reading glasses’ lenses (convex lenses) converged sharply at a single point, focusing precisely on the black plastic frame of the computer monitor.

What came to mind was the memory of an experiment we did in science class where we used a magnifying glass to burn black paper.
Fortunately, there was no damage to the display frame, but the thought of what might have happened if I hadn’t noticed and left it alone sent a chill down my spine. It was just one step away from what’s known as a “convergence fire.”

## Lesson

The scary thing about the low winter sun is that light streams in from angles you’d never expect during the summer.
It seems I should either adjust where I place my reading glasses on the stand or get into the habit of putting them back in their case when I take them off. It was an unexpected pitfall lurking in the peaceful morning scenery.
