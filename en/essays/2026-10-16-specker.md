# How I Got Sucked into the World of Sound: Chasing the "Original Sound" on Ubuntu [Speakers Edition]

For the PC's front speakers, I used a YAMAHA NS-C110 center speaker for the enclosure and Pioneer TS-F1030 car speakers for the actual speaker units.
Since the NS-C110 features two 10-cm drivers, my goal was to bypass the crossover and drive two TS-F1030s simultaneously, thereby using high-output speakers to reproduce the PC’s audio.
I was satisfied with this setup for a while, but as I grew accustomed to the sound, the lack of resolution began to bother me.
I had considered buying new speakers, but used models like the Yamaha NS-10MMT and the ONKYO D-NX10 were available at relatively affordable prices, so I tried bidding on them several times.
That’s when I found an NS-M325 listed at a bargain price. After looking into it, it seemed like the perfect size to place next to my PC monitor, so I immediately won the auction.

Today, I connected it to the TSS-20 and played some music. The bass—which I’d been worried about—came through clearly, and the sound quality was very satisfying.

Current Setup
PC: Gigabyte Technology Co., Ltd. H87-HD3
OS: Ubuntu Desktop 26.10
DAC, Amplifier: TSS-20
Front Speakers: YAMAHA NS-M325
Subwoofer: YAMAHA TSS-20
Rear Speakers: YAMAHA TSS-20
