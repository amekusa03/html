# It’s not that I’m cheap—I just like getting a good deal — The Shipping Cost Hurdle Surrounding Soldering Flux

October 17, 2026 / Daily Life / Crafts

## The Crucial Difference Between Being Cheap and Getting a Good Deal

I like getting good deals.

Even when buying at the list price, they’ll doggedly hunt down a 10% off coupon, crunch the numbers to figure out how a bundle deal effectively saves them the cost of two items, and time their purchase to coincide with a points bonus before hitting the checkout button. There’s a sense that they actually enjoy the process of strategizing—figuring out how to use their wits and ingenuity to get the best possible deal.

But please don't get me wrong. I'm definitely not "stingy."

I don’t just want to tighten my purse strings and end up wasting money on cheap items. If I recognize the value of something or am convinced that a tool is of high quality, I’m happy to pay for it, even if it costs a bit more.

What I love isn't "cheapness" itself, but rather "the sense of satisfaction that comes from getting a good deal through ingenuity" (= the feeling of getting a good deal).

## The Challenge of Flux, Which Costs Just a Few Hundred Yen

What’s really shaking up my sense of aesthetics right now is the purchase of “soldering flux,” an essential item for electronics projects.

A reliable partner that removes the oxide layer from circuit boards and dramatically improves solder wettability. Since my current supply was running low, I decided to order more of my go-to HOZAN flux online.

However, this is where the “shipping fee trap” set by modern e-commerce rears its ugly head.

The actual retail price of flux itself is around 400 to 500 yen at most. It’s an extremely affordable consumable.
However, when you actually try to buy it online, no matter where you look, shipping costs of around 500 to 660 yen are added on. The absurdity of “shipping costs being higher than the price of the item” is too much of a psychological blow for anyone who prides themselves on getting a good deal.

You might think, “Then why not just buy it on Amazon with free shipping?” But the condition Amazon set was “minimum purchase: 2 tubes.”
How many years do you think it would take to use up a single tube of flux for a home electronics project? If I bought two tubes, they’d practically become a lifelong legacy. That would amount to an actual expense of over 800 yen, which is clearly at odds with the “sense of value” I’m looking for.

Before I knew it, I had stopped working and was browsing major e-commerce sites, completely absorbed in comparing prices.

In terms of the actual cost, it’s clear that buying two on Amazon (818 yen) is the cheapest option.
There’s also the classic workaround of rounding up other consumables at Monotaro to exceed the 3,500-yen threshold for free shipping, but I don’t really need any other tools right now. Buying 3,000 yen worth of unnecessary items just to save on shipping is the height of putting the cart before the horse.

## solution

I won't.

Just to save a few hundred yen—or rather, for that “sense of getting a good deal”—I’ve already been staring at my computer for nearly an hour.
The term “cost-effectiveness” (or “time performance”) crosses my mind, but I feel like admitting that would be admitting defeat.

Once again today, I quietly closed the browser tab while leaving the Flux item in my Amazon cart with a quantity of "2."
